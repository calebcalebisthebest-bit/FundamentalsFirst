The Complete Shooting Guide

Master every aspect of basketball shooting with this comprehensive guide.

Why Shooting Matters

Shooting is the most important offensive skill in basketball. Every team needs players who can consistently put the ball in the basket from various distances and angles. Whether you're a point guard setting up the offense or a forward attacking the basket, developing a reliable shooting stroke will elevate your game and give your team more scoring options.

In this guide, you'll learn the technical aspects of shooting, common mistakes to avoid, and practice drills to help you become a more consistent, confident scorer.

The B.E.E.F Method Breakdown

Balance - The Foundation

Proper foot positioning is the foundation of all great shooting. Your feet should be:

  • Shoulder-width apart (or slightly wider)
  • With shooting-side foot slightly forward
  • Weight distributed evenly on the balls of your feet
  • Knees bent at approximately 45 degrees

Your stance should feel natural and comfortable, allowing you to generate power from your legs and maintain balance through the entire shot.

Eyes - Your Target Lock

Your eyes control your accuracy. Follow these guidelines:

  • Focus on the back of the rim throughout the entire shot
  • Try to shoot through the back of the rim rather than above it
  • Don't watch the ball—trust your mechanics
  • Adjust for depth perception with closer shots

Elite shooters use a "rim target" technique. Imagine a ring around the back of the rim and try to shoot through it with every shot.

Elbow - Your Release Point

Your elbow position determines the trajectory of your shot:

  • Keep your elbow tucked in close to your body
  • Form a 90-degree angle between upper arm and forearm
  • Your shooting arm should form a straight line from shoulder to wrist
  • Avoid flaring your elbow out to the side
  • The elbow should be directly under the ball

Think of your elbow as a hinge that guides the ball upward. A tucked elbow creates consistency and reduces the variables in your shot.

Follow Through - The Finish

Your follow-through determines shot rotation and arc:

  • Snap your wrist downward (like reaching into a cookie jar)
  • Extend your arm fully toward the basket
  • Hold your follow-through until the shot goes in
  • Your index and middle fingers should be the last to leave the ball
  • Your hand finishes with palm facing down

A good follow-through creates backspin on the ball, which helps it to softly arc toward the basket rather than bounce off hard.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Rushing Your Shot

Taking shots too quickly without proper setup leads to inconsistency. Always take time to gather the ball and align your body before shooting.

❌ Flat Release

Shooting flat (parallel to the ground) reduces your margin for error. Aim for a 45-52 degree arc for optimal shooting percentages.

❌ Off-Balance Shots

Shooting while off-balance drastically reduces accuracy. Always reset your feet and find your balance before taking the shot.

❌ Looking Away Too Soon

Don't watch the flight of the ball instead of the rim. Keep your eyes on the target through the entire release.

❌ Varying Release Point

Consistency comes from always releasing from the same point. Practice shooting from the same positions with the same mechanics.

❌ Muscling the Ball

Using arm strength instead of mechanics leads to poor form. Focus on proper technique rather than force.

Shooting Drills to Master

Close-Range Form Shooting

Build shooting confidence and consistency from close distance.

View Drill

Setup:

  • Position yourself 3 feet from the basket
  • Take 10 shots from this distance
  • Make all 10 before moving back

Progression:

  1. Start at 3 feet (make 10)
  2. Move to 6 feet (make 10)
  3. Move to free-throw line (make 10)
  4. Move to 3-point line (make 10)

Spot Shooting

Develop consistency from multiple shooting spots on the court.

View Drill

The 5 Spots:

  1. Wing (left side)
  2. Wing (right side)
  3. Corner (left side)
  4. Corner (right side)
  5. Top of the key

How to Execute:

Start at one spot. Take 5 shots and count your makes. Move to the next spot. Track your shooting percentage at each spot.

Catch and Shoot

Develop game-speed shooting off the catch.

View Drill

Partner Setup:

Work with a partner. Have them pass to you rapidly at different spots. Focus on quick release and accuracy.

Key Focus:

  • Catch with feet ready to shoot
  • Rise into the shot immediately
  • Don't dribble before shooting
